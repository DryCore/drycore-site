What the screen means

A program director read it and said continue.

NSF screens Project Pitches before allowing a full proposal. Most do not clear that screen. An invitation means a program director reviewed the concept, spoke with the founder, and judged the work in scope and worth reviewing properly.

It is not an award and it is not funding. Anyone evaluating this company should read it as exactly what it is: an independent technical reader, with no stake in the outcome, deciding the question was worth asking.

Why this program, and not capital

  • It buys instrumentsPhase I money pays for measurement, not for a commercial organization.
  • It is non-dilutiveNo equity is sold to find out whether the mechanism works.
  • It is reviewed by engineersThe proposal is judged by people who can read a measurement plan.
  • It sets the bar in publicThe pass and fail line goes into the proposal before any data exists.

Proposed scope

An experiment, not a facility.

The founder failure mode in a program like this is proposing a building when the reviewer asked for an experiment. Phase I retires one gating technical risk and characterizes the machine that depends on it.

01

Map the film

Measure continuity, coverage, and heat transfer across the operating envelope, against the pass and fail line written before the first run. This is the gate. If it fails, nothing downstream matters.

02

Characterize the machine

Measure how the rotor is driven across turndown, and whether the control system detects a failing film faster than the industry-standard method.

03

Calibrate the model

Replace the weakest modelled assumption with measured values, with every parasitic energy draw accounted for and uncertainty stated.

04

Safety, in the proposal

Natural refrigerants carry real hazards. Detection, charge limits, and hazards removed by architecture are written into the proposal, not bolted on after an award.
